Call for Papers

We welcome a wide range of contributions — including new methods, applications, analyses, benchmarks, and position pieces — from essentially any scientific domain, including biology, chemistry, physics, materials science, and climate science, that bring together machine learning and scientific simulators. We especially encourage work that incorporates real-world data, though submissions are not limited to it.

Submit Your Paper

Submissions are handled through OpenReview. Submission deadline: Aug 29, 2026Sep 2, 2026 (23:59 AoE).

The submission deadline has passed — see adding an author after the deadline.

Submission Tracks

1. Workshop Papers (5 pages)

We invite 5-page workshop papers on methodological advances, domain-specific applications, analyses, or other contributions that bridge simulators or emulators with real-world data using machine learning. Original contributions and early research works are encouraged. Contributions presenting recently published or currently under review results are also welcome.

2. Tiny Papers (2 pages)

We encourage 2-page extended abstracts (Tiny Papers), especially from under-represented, under-resourced, and budding researchers. These short contributions should address the same topics as workshop papers but in a more concise format, making research accessible to a broader community of participants. We hold Tiny Papers to a high quality bar and accept only exceptional submissions.

Contribution Guide

  • Format: Use the official NeurIPS 2026 LaTeX template with the dblblindworkshop package option and set the workshop title to Sim2Science, i.e. \usepackage[dblblindworkshop]{neurips_2026} and \workshoptitle{Sim2Science}. This is required for the camera-ready version and recommended already for submission. Submissions not using the required template may be desk-rejected.
  • Length: 5 pages (excluding references) for workshop papers, or 2 pages (excluding references) for Tiny Papers
  • Appendix: no page limit on supplementary/appendix material, but reviewers are not obligated to read it — your main submission (within the page limit) should be self-contained
  • Anonymization: Double-blind review required — please anonymize your submission (no author names/affiliations, avoid self-identifying references); failure to anonymize, or to follow the required template, may lead to desk rejection
  • Checklist: submissions must include the standard NeurIPS reproducibility checklist (provided as checklist.tex in the template), placed after the references and before the appendix; it does not count toward the page limit. Answer each item yes/no/NA with a brief justification. Missing or incomplete checklists may lead to desk rejection.
  • Submission Portal: OpenReview

Review Process

We use reciprocal reviewing, in line with how NeurIPS and other major ML venues run their review processes:

  • Nomination: at submission time, authors nominate one author of the paper — someone with sufficient relevant expertise and publications — to serve as the paper's reciprocal reviewer for the workshop. If no co-author has a strong publication record, authors may instead nominate a co-author with sufficient relevant expertise; this nominee is held to the same reviewing standard as any other reciprocal reviewer (see Participation below).
  • Invitation: at least one nominated reviewer must accept the reviewing invitation in OpenReview; if none does, the submission is subject to desk rejection.
  • Load: each nominated reviewer is assigned 2 other submissions to review.
  • Assignment: reviewers are assigned papers based on their expertise; assignments may be adjusted by organizers for coverage or conflicts of interest.
  • Participation: if a nominated reviewer fails to submit their reviews by the review deadline, or submits highly insufficient or inappropriate reviews, the corresponding submission is subject to desk rejection.

Reviewing is supplemented by program committee members and organizers as needed. Papers are reviewed double-blind.

For reviewers: we follow the NeurIPS 2026 Reviewer Guidelines, with two workshop-specific points:

  1. Judge a submission on its main text — it must be self-contained, and reviewers are not obligated to read the appendix.
  2. The reciprocal-reviewing rules above apply: if you do not accept the invitation or do not deliver adequate reviews on time, the submission that nominated you is subject to desk rejection.

Non-archival: The workshop will not have formal proceedings, but accepted submissions will be linked on the workshop webpage.

FAQ

Do I need a full paper to submit, or is an abstract enough?

Full 5-page (workshop paper) or 2-page (Tiny Paper) submissions are required by the submission deadline — we do not have a separate abstract-only deadline.

Can I add an author to my submission after the deadline?

After the submission deadline, we add new authors only for compelling reasons. To request an addition, email sim2science@gmail.com and include all of the following:

  • the OpenReview submission ID (and paper title);
  • the new author’s OpenReview profile;
  • the full author order as it should appear after the change;
  • the reason for the addition.

Incomplete requests cannot be processed. All decisions are at the organizers’ discretion.

Can I attend without submitting a paper?

Yes. The workshop is open to all registered NeurIPS 2026 attendees, with or without a submission.

Is the workshop archival? Can I submit this work elsewhere later?

Sim2Science is non-archival — there are no formal proceedings, so accepted work remains eligible for submission to archival venues afterward.

Can I submit work that is already published or under review elsewhere?

Yes. We welcome recently published results and work currently under review, alongside original contributions.

Can I submit a paper written or generated by an AI system?

Submissions must reflect substantive human intellectual contribution. Papers that are wholly AI/autonomous-system-generated are not eligible. Use of AI writing assistance should follow the NeurIPS 2026 policy on LLM use.

Will my submission be made public if it isn't accepted?

No — submissions are kept confidential unless accepted and the authors confirm inclusion in the workshop. Rejected or withdrawn submissions are not made public.

Is there a code of conduct for the workshop?

Yes — all authors and attendees are expected to comply with the NeurIPS Code of Conduct.

How can I get involved beyond submitting a paper?

For ways to get involved, or with questions or interest, reach out to sim2science@gmail.com.
